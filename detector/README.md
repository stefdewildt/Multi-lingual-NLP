In [Mireshghallah et al.](literature/2305.09859v4.pdf) they evaluate all kinds detection models: smaller, larger, architecture, training data, etc. Their conclusion is that the decector works best when the fake texts are generated by a model that's similar to the detector model in terms of training data and architecture. And notably: smaller detector models perform better than larger ones. 

We'll be focussing less on the architectural side, but more on the (training) data side, and more specifically on the languages in the training data. We'd still have to check multiple architectures (to eliminate bias), but to a lesser extend. For every architecture we choose, we'll be needing pretrained versions that are trained on diffent datasets in terms of linguality. 

* A version trained on only english
* A version trained on most seen languages (english, french, spanish, portuguese)
* A version also trained on less seen languages (dutch, mongolian, etc)
